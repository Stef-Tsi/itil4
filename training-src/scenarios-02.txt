2.1|Guiding principles|A team must choose between two useful guiding principles during an improvement. What is the BEST approach?|Consider how all relevant principles work together in this context|Assign each principle to a separate department|Choose one principle permanently and ignore the others|Apply the principles only after implementation|Principles are intended to support decisions together and remain relevant across changing circumstances. They are not exclusive department rules or a checklist reserved for the end of work.|Principes werken samen. Je hoeft niet permanent één principe te kiezen. Kijk welke principes relevant zijn en hoe ze elkaar ondersteunen in deze situatie.|Consider them together.
2.1|Guiding principles|What makes a guiding principle useful when an organization changes its tools or management structure?|It provides broadly applicable guidance rather than a tool-specific procedure|It prescribes the same implementation steps for every organization|It assigns technical ownership of each software component|It defines the service level agreement for the new tools|Guiding principles give direction across circumstances; they are not fixed implementation procedures. Ownership details and service agreements need context-specific design, informed but not replaced by the principles.|Een guiding principle geeft richting, geen stappenplan voor één tool. Daarom blijft het bruikbaar als structuur of technologie verandert. De invulling moet nog steeds passen bij de situatie.|Guidance across circumstances, not a fixed procedure.
2.2|Focus on value|A support manager wants to reduce average call duration. Which question should be asked BEFORE optimizing that metric?|Will shorter calls help users achieve their intended results?|Can a stricter timer be added to each support call?|Can complex calls be excluded from the reported average?|Which system can automatically close long calls?|The target should serve users' outcomes and experience. Shorter calls may help, but may also create repeat contacts if issues are rushed. Tooling and reporting changes should follow an understanding of value, not replace it.|Kortere gesprekken zijn niet vanzelf beter. Als het probleem niet wordt opgelost, belt de gebruiker opnieuw. Begin bij het gewenste resultaat en gebruikerservaring; kies daarna een passende metric en oplossing.|Optimize for value, not an isolated number.
2.2|Start where you are|A dashboard says requests are completed quickly, but staff report days of waiting. What should a team do FIRST?|Observe actual work and check whether the measurements reflect it|Discard all measurements and rely only on opinions|Replace the workflow before examining current activities|Assume the dashboard is correct because it is automated|Direct observation and validated measurement help establish the real current state. Measurements can omit queues or handoffs; anecdotes also need checking. Neither blind trust nor automatic rejection is appropriate.|Bekijk hoe het werk echt verloopt en controleer wat de metric wel en niet meet. Wachtrijen kunnen buiten beeld blijven. Blind vertrouwen op de tool én alle data afwijzen zijn beide onjuist.|Observe and validate the baseline.
2.2|Progress iteratively with feedback|What is missing when a project uses small delivery batches but postpones all evaluation until the end?|Feedback that informs subsequent increments|A mandatory central change advisory board|A requirement to replace every existing tool|A separate guiding principle for every batch|Small batches alone are not enough. Feedback must be used early enough to affect subsequent decisions. It can be gathered before, during and after iterations, not only in a final review.|Kleine stukken leveren is maar de helft. De feedback moet nog invloed kunnen hebben op volgende stappen. Alleen een eindbeoordeling levert geen tussentijdse bijsturing op.|Iterate and learn, not just split the work.
2.2|Collaborate and promote visibility|Which action BEST improves collaboration without requiring everyone to approve every decision?|Involve stakeholders whose knowledge or needs are relevant to the decision|Require unanimous approval from every employee|Keep decisions private until every task is finished|Replace all human discussion with a reporting tool|Useful collaboration involves the right contributions and clear information. Universal consensus can delay work; hiding decisions harms visibility. A tool may support collaboration but does not replace it.|Betrek mensen met relevante kennis of belangen. Iedereen laten stemmen over alles kan juist vertragen. Een dashboard helpt, maar vervangt samenwerking en duidelijke beslissingsbevoegdheid niet.|Right involvement, not universal consensus.
2.2|Think and work holistically|A team measures only its own processing time and ignores the time work waits elsewhere. What should be considered to improve the whole service?|End-to-end dependencies, queues and stakeholder outcomes|Only the number of tasks that team closes|Only the team's resource utilization|Only the technical speed of its local tool|A service crosses organizational and technical boundaries. Improving local utilization or task counts can worsen overall flow. Holistic thinking considers the whole system and its results, including waiting time.|De service loopt over teamgrenzen heen. Een team kan efficiënt lijken terwijl het werk elders vastloopt. Kijk daarom naar de totale route en uitkomsten, niet alleen naar lokale bezetting of snelheid.|The whole service includes the handoffs.
2.2|Keep it simple and practical|A safety review shows that a control is essential, but the form supporting it asks for irrelevant duplicate data. What is the BEST response?|Keep the necessary control and simplify the unnecessary form fields|Remove the control because simplicity means having fewer controls|Keep every field because some control is necessary|Automate the form without checking its purpose|Simplicity means removing unnecessary complexity while still achieving objectives and necessary controls. Keeping all paperwork is not required; removing essential protection or automating duplication is not a sound interpretation.|Behoud de noodzakelijke controle, maar haal overbodige velden weg. Simpel betekent niet dat je relevante beveiliging moet schrappen. En automatisering maakt dubbele invoer niet ineens nuttig.|Simple enough, but still controlled.
2.2|Optimize and automate|Which activity should generally precede automating an inefficient workflow?|Understanding and improving the workflow where needed|Increasing the number of manual approvals|Buying a bot and preserving every existing step|Defining success only as fewer human interactions|Automation should support a fit-for-purpose, optimized way of working. It does not make unnecessary steps valuable. The objective is useful outcomes with appropriate resources, not eliminating humans regardless of context.|Begrijp en verbeter de werkwijze voordat je die automatiseert. Het doel is goed werk en waarde, niet koste wat kost mensen weghalen. Een slecht proces sneller uitvoeren blijft een slecht proces.|Improve the work before automating it.
2.2|Focus on value|A team automates a task flawlessly, but the task is no longer needed by any stakeholder. What is the central lesson?|Efficiency in work that creates no useful value is not enough|Automation is incompatible with service management|Every existing task must be retained as part of governance|Technical accuracy is the complete definition of value|A technically accurate and efficient task may still be unnecessary. Focus on value helps decide whether work should exist at all. This does not reject automation or governance; it links them to relevant objectives.|De uitvoering kan foutloos zijn en toch niets opleveren. Vraag eerst of de taak nog nodig is. Automatisering en governance zijn niet het probleem; het ontbreken van nuttige waarde is dat wel.|Doing an unnecessary task well is still unnecessary.
