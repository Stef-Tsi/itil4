4.1|Service value system|What are opportunity and demand in relation to the SVS?|Inputs that can be transformed into value through the system|Two of the six service value chain activities|Two replacement names for utility and warranty|Outputs that always guarantee customer satisfaction|Opportunity and demand are inputs to the SVS. 4.1|Service value system|Why should an organization avoid managing SVS components as disconnected silos?|Value creation depends on how the components and activities work together|Each component must have an identical detailed workflow|The SVS requires all decisions to be made by one department|Governance removes the need for collaboration between practices|The SVS emphasizes the system of interactions. 5.1|Value streams|Which statement about the six value chain activities is MOST accurate?|They can be combined and revisited in ways appropriate to a value stream|They must occur exactly once in a fixed sequence|They are six separate organizational departments|They replace the need for practices|Value chain activities are interconnected, flexible building blocks. 5.1|Practices and activities|A practice helps work in several value chain activities.
